## DZztwi6Gxfn
A few posts ago I taught everyone how to turn a terrible document into a presentation-ready slide deck with 6 prompts, but prompts get forgotten, pasted incompletely, and redone from scratch every time. Now I just type one sentence: "Turn this into a presentation for me." It takes inventory on its own, intercepts personal data, recalculates the figures to catch errors, generates, and outputs a perfect PPT — that is a skill
ppt-creator skill: github.com/Yu-03…
Takeaway ①: What is a skill?
A skill "saves" a professional's thinking steps, triggered with one sentence and applied automatically
Takeaway ②: The basic design
It opens with a questionnaire, and once you've filled it in, it runs all the way through
After you drop in your files, it first gives you a short setup questionnaire:
1. Material source: ① existing documents ② only a topic (it searches the web for you) ③ continue from the last progress
2. Who it's for
3. What occasion it will be used for
4. Presentation-style or reading-style
5. Canvas size (16:9 / portrait / A4…)
6. Whether there's a brand style ("make it look nicer" is garbage-in, and the skill automatically translates it into specs: a one-line mood, color codes (≤2 accents per page), serif headings + sans-serif body text, layouts matched to content, no tables)
7. Whether you want to see the design spec first
Once you've filled it in, it runs straight to the end and doesn't keep turning back to bother you at every step.
---
Takeaway ③: Designs that keep it stable and error-free
The questionnaire is only the entrance; what really makes it "stable" is the following designs:
① Filter content → define specs → lay out and output: finish one layer before moving to the next. The AI most easily starts laying things out before it has understood the content (forced layering is there to prevent this)
② "Hard stops" where it should stop: certain steps are set as hard stops that must wait for your response before continuing — the AI isn't allowed to make things up and keep going, nor to sneak-write HTML during the planning stage. This is the key to why it won't "go off on its own and finish a deck you didn't want at all"
③ Three points are marked as "not for the AI to decide," such as whether to reveal the holding area, how to handle inconsistent data, and acceptance of the visual result. These three are matters of judgment, responsibility, and outcomes, but we still keep the bottom line of "AI mass-produces, a human gatekeeps"
④ By default, names, salaries, and non-compete clauses are intercepted into the holding area to ensure information security, and every block of the original text must be tagged with where it went — no content is allowed to silently disappear. It would rather ask more questions and let you decide than quietly leak anything.

## DZoFMp-k6qp
"In the AI era, what can someone who can't code possibly use to compete with engineers?"
A 3-minute rundown of this underdog's playbook
My last post covered "how to write a precise prompt"
But having to write something this complete every time → exhausting, I can't remember the format, and copying it every time is a hassle
So this thread covers only one thing: how to make AI stronger on its own
Takeaway ①: α and Ω — let your prompts evolve themselves
You only need two fixed instructions:
α (the generator): a prompt that specializes in "generating" other prompts
For example:
"You are a prompt engineer. I will give you a task requirement, and you must output a high-quality, ready-to-use prompt.
Requirements:
1. Begin by defining the role and the goal
2. List clear steps
3. Specify the output format
4. Add boundary conditions (what not to do)
My requirement: ＿＿＿＿ "
Ω (the optimizer): a prompt that specializes in "optimizing" other prompts
For example:
"You are a prompt review expert. I will give you a prompt.
Please:
1. Point out its 3 weaknesses (vague, missing role, no format constraints…)
2. Give a complete revised new version
3. Explain why each change is better
The prompt to optimize: ＿＿＿＿ "
---
The way to play is a loop:
1️⃣ Have the AI first write initial versions of α and Ω for you
2️⃣ Use Ω to optimize α → get a stronger α
3️⃣ Use the new α to generate all the prompts you use day to day
4️⃣ Feed the results back in and optimize another round
Every time you run a cycle, your prompt library automatically levels up. You're no longer writing prompts — you're raising a prompt factory that evolves on its own
Takeaway ②: 7 AI house rules for beginners
1. If you're unsure, ask me; do not guess my intent
2. For complex tasks, first list a TODO list and work on one item at a time
3. Follow the "understand → plan → execute → verify" loop, and don't skip steps
4. Look at what already exists first; don't start from scratch out of thin air
5. Before changing anything, explain the purpose and the impact
6. Prioritize technical correctness; don't pander to me (the AI's biggest flaw is going along with whatever you say — this rule cures it at the root) 7. Keep answers concise, with no pleasantries or filler

Takeaway ①: Garbage in, garbage out
First principle: context is everything. Garbage in, garbage out
The AI can't read minds. Your need has 100 points of detail in your head, you only say 10 of them, and the AI makes up the other 90 with imagination — then you blame it for making things up.
So the essence of a precise prompt isn't "wording tricks" — it's fully moving the context in your head over to the AI.
Remember this sentence; everything that follows revolves around it.
Takeaway ②: The 4 components of a precise prompt
The methodology condensed into one formula, applicable to any kind of work:
Precise prompt = Goal + Non-goals + Boundaries + Acceptance criteria
🎯 Goal: say in one sentence what you want 　"Write me a weekly report for my manager, focusing on progress and risks"
🚫 Non-goals: say explicitly what you don't want 　"No more than 300 words, no pleasantries"
🧱 Boundaries: what can be touched and what can't 　"Only edit the second paragraph and leave the other paragraphs as they are"
✅ Acceptance criteria: what counts as done 　"When finished, list which 3 places you changed"
Missing any one component gives the AI that much more room to freelance.
