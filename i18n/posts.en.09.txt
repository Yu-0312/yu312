## DcP4Sz4E3rL
OpenAI is playing an important card in the enterprise market: even when using the most powerful frontier models, enterprises don't have to give up control over their confidential data
The more a model can run long workflows and work autonomously across many interactions, the more security risk is likely to hide in a chain of requests rather than in a single conversation. OpenAI's solution is Private Safety Processing: it lets automated systems recognize patterns of abuse across interactions, without OpenAI staff directly seeing the enterprise's prompts and responses.
The enterprise keeps control of its data, and OpenAI receives only limited safety alerts.
But don't get the wrong idea: this doesn't mean the conversations of all ChatGPT users instantly become "zero data retention." For now it mainly targets eligible, approved API/enterprise customers.
This isn't just a privacy policy — it's a race for trust in enterprise AI: who can make enterprises dare to hand over their most important data while the models get more powerful?

## DcCjMnemanu
Knowledge transfer is being completely upended in the age of AI. The thread below is going to take a slice of AI instructors' cake — savor it with care
Traditional knowledge transfer and learning always go like this: listen to the teacher's lecture ➔ go home with an empty head ➔ when you actually need to use it, there's too much information to grasp the key points
The real beginning of learning has never been what you hear in class, but "the moment you apply what you learned to your own situation."
The biggest change in the AI era is that the cost of personalization has dropped dramatically. In the past, limited by manpower, it wasn't possible to provide personalized service to everyone.
The biggest pain points in delivering knowledge are:
1. One-on-one coaching of learners ➔ can't be delivered at scale
2. Content is generic, sprinkled thin like pepper powder over everyone ➔ learners can't actually learn it
How does the AI era solve this? Personalization can run through every stage of learning
1️⃣ Personalized learning materials
When signing up for events and courses abroad, learners provide information such as their professional background and job content. Based on this information, AI can generate a customized initial set of learning materials for each person.
Everyone's starting point is no longer the same; each person begins learning from where they actually stand
Even the tools learners are used to are taken into account in the personalization
Not everyone is on the same starting line, which makes it easier for learners to apply and understand
---
2️⃣ Personalized guidance during the course
In the past it was impossible to assign every learner a customized teaching assistant:
1. TAs are hard to train
2. Abilities are uneven
3. They'd have to learn the whole course first
The AI-era solution: the course's methodology is internalized directly into a "skill," which is simply installed in the learner's own workbench. When learners practice, the AI on their own computer can give personalized guidance and answers in real time, acting as a personal teaching assistant.
This forms a "three-teacher model":
Lead instructor ➔ teaches the course overall
The AI on the learner's own computer ➔ personal, individualized guidance
The instructor-side agent ➔ keeps track in real time of the whole class's progress and sticking points, and judges whether a human TA needs to step in to help
3️⃣ What's delivered shifts from "teaching materials" to "results"
In the past, after training you took home handouts, notes, and videos, and then had to digest them yourself and turn them into something actually usable
Now, during the course, learners directly build an AI workflow for their own business based on their own work
What they take away at the end is:
✅ A pre-installed system that works right out of the box
✅ A dedicated user manual explaining the directions in which this workflow can be extended
